Methodology

These ratings are derived from the betting market. Closing prices are turned into an expected number of goals for each match, and a statistical model estimates the attacking and defensive strength those prices imply for every team. They update daily as new prices come in.

A team’s rating is its attack plus defence. Within a league the numbers rank teams directly; the global ladder places every league on one comparable, cross-league scale.

Season projection beta

The Leagues page projects where each club is likely to finish. Every remaining fixture is simulated many times from the same market-implied ratings — a plausible score is drawn for each side — and the final tables are tallied into a finishing-position distribution, the shaded strip, from which Title, Top 4 and Relegation probabilities follow. This is experimental and not yet independently validated: unlike the ratings, it has not been checked against held-out results, a just-promoted club with no market history is treated as its league’s weakest existing side, and relegation play-off berths are not modelled. Treat it as an illustration, not a settled forecast.

Frequently asked

How are these ratings calculated?

They are derived from the betting market. Closing prices are turned into an expected number of goals for each match, and a statistical model estimates the attacking and defensive strength those prices imply for every team. The ratings update every day as new prices come in.

What does a team's rating number mean?

A team's rating is its attack plus its defence, shown as a deviation from its league's average — a higher number is a stronger team. Within a league the numbers rank teams directly.

Are the ratings comparable across leagues?

Yes. The global ladder places every league on one common scale, so clubs from different leagues can be compared directly; the per-league tables rank clubs within a single league.

How often are the ratings updated?

Every day. Each page shows when it was last rebuilt, and the ratings reflect the most recent closing prices available.

What does the ! badge next to a club's name mean?

It flags a club whose rating is meaningfully less certain than most, usually because the market hasn't priced many of its games recently — a newly promoted side, or one with few European fixtures. Hovering the badge shows the 95% range the true strength very likely sits in, or says so when there isn't enough data to estimate one at all. Most clubs carry no badge: the model is reasonably confident in their rating. The uncertainty reflects fitting the ratings, not day-to-day swings in form, so treat it as a floor on the real uncertainty.
